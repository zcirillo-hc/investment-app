# Theme

Written 2026-09-09 from the owner's answers. This is the through-line the product is built
around. It is not a style guide, it is a set of commitments that decide what gets built.

---

## 1. Who this is for, precisely

18 to 25, and specifically the ones who have said all three of these:

1. **"I do not have a lot of money."** Not a complaint about being broke, a statement of fact
   about capacity. They cannot spare $100 a month and every product that assumes they can has
   already told them it is not for them.
2. **"I am scared of losing it."** The money is small and it therefore matters more, not less.
   Losing $40 is not trivial when $40 is what you have.
3. **"I do not know how to start."** Not a knowledge gap about markets, a procedural one. They
   do not know what a brokerage is, what opening one involves, or which button comes first.

These are the only three obstacles this product exists to remove. A feature that does not
remove one of them is decoration.

## 2. What success looks like

In the owner's words: **"I have a habit and I saved $X."**

So the product optimizes for a repeated choice and an accumulating number, in that order.
It does NOT optimize for knowledge in the abstract, or for getting somebody to open a
brokerage account by a deadline. Those follow from the habit. The habit comes first.

## 3. The three commitments

### 3.1 Against "I do not have a lot of money"

- **The unit of action is one skipped purchase.** Never a deposit, never a monthly amount,
  never a target contribution. The app never asks for money the user has to find.
- **Small numbers are the hero, not an apology.** $4.35 is displayed as a win, at full size,
  with no hedging. Never "only", never "just", never a bigger number placed next to it to make
  it look small.
- **No number on screen may imply the user needs more than they have.** This is why the
  summer chart and the your-money chart live on separate scales: a real $27 next to a
  hypothetical $98,000 tells the user they are failing.

### 3.2 Against "I am scared of losing it"

- **The app never shows a number going down.** It does not track market value, so it cannot.
  This started as an honesty decision and it turns out to be the fear answer too: there is no
  red number here to panic at.
- **Risk is taught as time, not as danger.** The honest question is never "how risky is this",
  it is "when do you need the money". That framing is already in the Learn library and it is
  the spine of everything written about risk.
- **Nothing is locked.** Say plainly and often that the money is theirs and can come back out.
  Fear of loss is partly fear of being trapped.

### 3.3 Against "I do not know how to start"

- **Procedural, not conceptual.** What a brokerage is, what they ask you for, what the first
  move looks like, what the fees are. Steps in order, not theory.
- **The app is a rehearsal, not the real thing, and says so.** All spending is simulated and
  the ledger is what the user typed. Being honest about that is what makes it safe to practise
  in, which is the entire value of the rehearsal.
- **Never name a security, never tell them what to buy.** R15 in full. Removing the fear of
  starting must not become telling somebody what to start with.

## 4. Voice

The owner's words: warm and playful, knowledgeable and credible, a little funny, not preachy,
clear and defined, a joke here and there to keep people on their toes.

That resolves to four rules:

1. **Clear first.** If a sentence has to choose between funny and clear, it is clear. The
   audience's problem is confusion, and a joke that costs comprehension is a net loss.
2. **Credible by being specific.** Vague reassurance reads as marketing. "Opening an account
   takes about fifteen minutes and is mostly typing" earns more trust than "it is easy".
3. **Funny occasionally and never at the user.** The joke lands on the situation, the industry
   or the app itself. Never on the person reading it, and never on being broke.
4. **Not preachy, which means no imperatives about their money.** Explain, do not instruct.
   This is also R15's line, so the voice rule and the compliance rule agree.

## 5. What this theme forbids

- Streaks that break. A broken streak is a punishment, and punishing somebody for missing a
  day contradicts every one of the three commitments. Count what they did, never what they
  failed to do.
- Any number presented as a shortfall, a gap, or behind schedule.
- Comparison to other users.
- Any suggestion that the amount they can spare is too small to bother with.
- Urgency. Nothing here expires.
